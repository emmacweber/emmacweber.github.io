About

I am a PhD candidate in Applied Mathematics, with a dual M.S. in Data Science, at the University of Delaware, advised by Dr. Dominique Guillot. I am interested in using machine learning to accelerate computational simulations and improve their accuracy. In my research I have used graph neural networks and physics-informed neural networks to model physical systems, with applications in climate science and fluid flow.

As these models are used more, I am also interested in how we evaluate them, especially physics emulators. More broadly, I enjoy the mathematics behind these methods, including the transferability of graph neural networks and manifold learning.

Research

UD · Thesis · 2022–present

Geometric graph neural networks for physical fields

Reconstructing historical near-surface temperature on the sphere from sparse, irregular observations. In a distance-aware graph attention network, each grid cell is connected to its nearest neighbors by great-circle distance, so neighborhoods are defined by physical distance rather than by grid index. It attains the lowest error of the four methods compared, with the largest gains in the sparsely observed polar regions, and has 469 times fewer parameters than LaMa. Supported by an NSF ACCESS allocation (PI).

PNNL · NSIP intern · Summer 2026

Reference-free evaluation of physics emulators

During a summer internship at PNNL, I developed a way to evaluate physics-informed neural networks using only the residuals of the governing equations, without needing a reference solution. A technical report is forthcoming.

Sandia · CSRI intern · Summer 2025

Hybrid neural ODEs for temperature forecasting

During a summer internship at Sandia, I built a hybrid neural ordinary differential equation model that pairs a simplified Earth system model with neural networks to forecast temperature. I presented this work at JMM and SIAM Annual in 2026.

Earlier work includes hybrid statistical and ML methods for paleoclimate reconstruction (UD, 2022), hurricane intensity modeling with dynamical systems and recurrent networks (UMass Amherst, 2020–21), noise, vibration and harshness reduction with Rivian engineers at the IMA Math-to-Industry Bootcamp (2024), and a collaboration on the mathematics of sea ice and polar ecosystems that grew out of the 2025 SLMath summer graduate school.

Service

Association for Women in Mathematics, UD Chapter

President (2024–25) · Vice President (2023–24) · Secretary (2022–23)

During my three years on the executive board, I worked to make the chapter a place where women and underrepresented groups in mathematics could find community and prepare for their careers. I organized panels and meet-and-greets, including our Job Application Series, and coordinated events that brought undergraduate and graduate students together. I also led the chapter’s Graduate Peer Mentorship Program, matching mentees with mentors and coordinating its events. On the executive board, I spearheaded the creation of two new roles: an Undergraduate Representative and a Social Media and Outreach Chair. In 2025, the chapter received an AWM Chapter Spotlight for its career preparation and academic success.
